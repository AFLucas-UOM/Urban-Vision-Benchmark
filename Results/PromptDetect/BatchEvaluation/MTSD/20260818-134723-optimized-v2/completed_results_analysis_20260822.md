# PromptDetect completed-results analysis

As-of: 2026-08-22, Europe/Malta. This analysis covers 198 completed combinations:

- MDWD targeted: 30/30
- MTSD targeted: 65/65
- MDWD prompt sensitivity: 80/80
- MTSD prompt sensitivity: 23/100 (incomplete)

The source of truth is each completed combination's `metrics.json`, generated by
the PromptDetect evaluator. The evaluator uses greedy one-to-one matching at
IoU 0.50. Aggregate precision, recall and F1 below are micro-aggregates over
all matched boxes; prompt-level averages are reported separately where useful.

## Technical summary

SAM 3.1 is the strongest completed model on MDWD targeted evaluation by
micro-F1 (0.5478), narrowly ahead of SAM 3 (0.5411). On MTSD targeted,
Cosmos Reason2 8B has the highest completed micro-F1 (0.3038), followed by
SAM 3 (0.2901) and SAM 3.1 (0.2863). The result is not a universal winner:
Cosmos 8B has lower MTSD recall than SAM 3 (0.3951 versus 0.4564) but higher
precision than SAM (0.2467 versus 0.2127), while SAM has a much better
precision/runtime balance than the VLMs.

The main cross-dataset finding is difficulty shift. Every model's F1 falls on
MTSD relative to MDWD, especially LocateAnything and the Cosmos 2B model.
MTSD's small, dense traffic-sign imagery produces many more false positives,
particularly for LocateAnything.

## Targeted model comparison

### MDWD targeted: SAM is the most balanced detector

| Model | Precision | Recall | F1 | mean IoU | mAP50-95* | ms/image |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SAM 3 | 0.5360 | 0.5464 | 0.5411 | 0.8891 | 0.4141 | 110.9 |
| SAM 3.1 | 0.5284 | 0.5687 | 0.5478 | 0.8894 | 0.4199 | 111.7 |
| LocateAnything 3B | 0.3206 | 0.7306 | 0.4456 | 0.8839 | 0.1846* | 2213.4 |
| Cosmos Reason2 8B | 0.4860 | 0.4467 | 0.4655 | 0.8541 | 0.1656* | 2357.4 |
| Cosmos Reason2 2B | 0.5115 | 0.2770 | 0.3594 | 0.8472 | 0.0827* | 1529.6 |

The totals behind these rates are important: MDWD contains 3,040 target GT
boxes per model across the six prompts. SAM 3.1 matched 1,729 boxes with
1,543 false positives and 1,311 misses. LocateAnything matched more boxes
(2,221) but generated 4,707 false positives. This is why its recall is strong
but its F1 remains below SAM.

Prompt difficulty is uneven. Orange garbage bags (`mdwd-p4`) are the hardest
target for every model; broad prompts (`mdwd-p5` and `mdwd-p6`) are much easier
in aggregate, with broad-prompt micro-F1 0.5072 versus 0.4131 for
class-targeted prompts. That comparison is descriptive, not a pure prompt
effect, because the broad prompts contain more target classes and more GT
boxes.

| LocateAnything 3B | 0.0922 | 0.4682 | 0.1541 | 0.9429 | 0.0402* | 7008.4 |

MTSD contains 5,703 target GT boxes per model across thirteen prompts. Cosmos
8B matched 2,253 boxes but produced 6,878 false positives; SAM 3 matched 2,603
with 9,637 false positives. LocateAnything's 26,289 false positives dominate
its result. Its mean matched IoU of 0.9429 is excellent conditional on a
match, but that number cannot compensate for the large number of incorrect
detections.

Prompt family matters strongly on MTSD:

| Prompt family | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Broad | 0.4020 | 0.3586 | 0.3791 |
| Class-targeted | 0.0871 | 0.4914 | 0.1480 |
| Synonym comparison | 0.0956 | 0.5765 | 0.1641 |

Broad prompts are not directly comparable to class-targeted prompts because
they explicitly target most or all traffic-control classes. The main signal
is that narrow and synonym prompts produce many false positives relative to
their true-positive yield. Street sign and directional sign are particularly
weak targets; broad `traffic sign` is much stronger.

## Prompt sensitivity findings

### MDWD sensitivity is complete and shows model-specific prompt effects

Across all 16 variants per model, micro-F1 ranks SAM 3 (0.5644) and SAM 3.1
(0.5639) first, followed by Cosmos 8B (0.3853), LocateAnything (0.2998), and
Cosmos 2B (0.2487).

For SAM, prompt form changes the precision/recall balance substantially:

- canonical: F1 0.6230 for SAM 3 and 0.6102 for SAM 3.1;
- lexical paraphrase: 0.6042 and 0.6024;
- descriptive paraphrase: 0.5403 and 0.5347;
- instruction: 0.4663 and 0.4973.

The instruction variant is not simply worse in every dimension: for SAM 3 it
raises precision to 0.7282 but lowers recall to 0.3430. Descriptive wording
does the opposite, raising recall to 0.7814 while reducing precision to
0.4129. This indicates a prompt-dependent operating-point shift, not just
random noise.

Cosmos 8B is comparatively stable on MDWD sensitivity: F1 ranges only from
0.3762 to 0.3941 across the four prompt styles. Cosmos 2B is less stable;
instruction wording reaches F1 0.3475 versus 0.2162 for the canonical form.
LocateAnything's lexical paraphrase is its best variant (F1 0.3524), while
instruction wording has high recall (0.8285) but low precision (0.1487).

### MTSD sensitivity is not complete yet

The completed MTSD sensitivity data currently consists of all 20 SAM 3
variants and only three SAM 3.1 variants. SAM 3's current aggregate is
precision 0.1113, recall 0.7243, F1 0.1930. Its canonical variants are much
better balanced (F1 0.3227) than lexical (0.1696), descriptive (0.1421), or
instruction variants (0.2274).

These MTSD sensitivity findings must not yet be generalized to all five models:
Cosmos 2B, Cosmos 8B, and LocateAnything have not completed their sensitivity
families, and SAM 3.1 has only three of twenty variants complete.

## How to interpret AP and mAP

The SAM AP/mAP values are meaningful because SAM emits real confidence scores.
SAM targeted mAP50-95 is approximately 0.414 for MDWD and 0.233 for MTSD,
with SAM 3 and SAM 3.1 essentially tied on MTSD.

Cosmos and LocateAnything do not emit calibrated per-box confidence. The
pipeline assigns a constant score of 1.0 and marks `ap_meaningful=false`.
Their AP50 and mAP50-95 values are retained for completeness but should not
be used to rank them against SAM. For these models, use precision, recall, F1,
false-positive counts, mean matched IoU, and runtime.

## Validation and limitations

The completed metrics are internally consistent with the evaluator's saved
definitions: fixed IoU 0.50 matching, explicit prompt target taxonomies,
test-set-derived 18-box cap, and separate target/non-target accounting.
The comparison is not yet final because 77 MTSD sensitivity combinations are
still outstanding at the time of this report.

The most important methodological limitation is macro versus micro weighting.
The tables above use micro precision/recall/F1 so large target classes and
prompt families contribute according to their GT-box volume. Prompt-level
macro averages are also saved in each `metrics.json`; they can differ when a
few rare classes are difficult. Neither view alone is sufficient for the
final dissertation conclusion.

## Recommended interpretation now

1. Treat SAM 3.1 as the best completed MDWD targeted model by F1 and meaningful
   mAP, with SAM 3 effectively tied.
2. Treat Cosmos 8B as the current MTSD targeted F1 leader, but not as a clear
   practical winner until its precision/runtime trade-off is compared with SAM
   and the completed sensitivity matrix.
3. Do not select LocateAnything based on mean IoU or recall alone; its false
   positives are the dominant failure mode on MTSD.
4. Do not use constant-confidence VLM mAP to rank models.
5. Wait for all MTSD sensitivity families to complete before making claims
   about the globally most robust prompt style.

remaining MTSD sensitivity combinations finish.
